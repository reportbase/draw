tvf galleries

Seventeen pages of live shapes, in reading order. Every shape on them is N samples of a band-limited loop and nothing else, and each page tests that one idea against something harder: why it works, symmetry, mirrors, a second coordinate system, shapes as points, the classical curves, terrain, a curve that never closes, growth, whole scenes, type, tilings, sound and motion, and then the page that says where it all breaks.

Every tile is live. Move its dials and the shape, its spectrum and the line of source code under it all update together.
The bars underneath are its spectrum. Bar m is how strongly the outline wobbles m times per lap, on a log scale, so an exact zero is a tick on the floor.
edit opens the shape in the leaf editor to sculpt. .tvf and svg copy it as text.
Test yourself. Turn on predict in a page's bar and each spectrum waits behind a question until you have guessed it.
link copies an address for a tile at its current settings, and the address bar always follows the last tile you touched.

The catalogue

Sixty-odd specimens and compositions, all built one way: a closed curve is N samples of a band-limited loop. This is the vocabulary: the shapes, the operations that bend them, and the objects made from them.

The bench

The catalogue shows what the format makes; this shows why it works, one piece at a time, with every number computed in front of you. One kernel, the sum that makes the copies add to exactly one, a signal, a shape, and step five: a mistake that leaves every sample right and everything between them wrong.
3 which harmonics 19 entries

Snow crystals

Symmetry is a statement about the spectrum. Six-fold symmetry forces every harmonic that is not a multiple of six to be exactly zero, so the leaf count becomes a specification rather than a quality setting. Too few leaves and a crystal comes back with the wrong number of arms.
4 what phases 21 entries

Radiolaria

Snowflakes say which harmonics may appear. Plankton add a second rule: mirror symmetry decides their phases. Reflect a shape onto itself and every sine term vanishes; break the mirror and the same harmonics acquire phase. That is the whole difference between Navicula and Pleurosigma.
5 a second chart 16 entries

Curves of constant width

Describe a curve by its support function, the distance to each tangent line, instead of its radius. Facts invisible in one chart become one line in the other: a curve has constant width exactly when its even harmonics vanish. Reuleaux triangles, the 20p coin, the Wankel rotor.
6 shapes as points 15 entries

Shape space

A shape with N leaves is 2N numbers, so a family of shapes is a cloud of points, and everything linear algebra knows about clouds applies: a mean, principal axes, a Gaussian to draw new members from. The catalogue's morph is a segment between two points; this is the whole space, and where it stops being flat.
7 the classical curves, by band 11 entries

The menagerie

The named curves, sorted by the one property this format cares about. Some are exactly a finite trigonometric sum, some converge faster than any power of 1/N, and some carry a singularity that fixes their convergence order forever. The surprise: the band belongs to the parameterization, not to the picture.
8 no last harmonic 17 entries

Landscape

The awkward case. A skyline is a graph, not a loop, and rough terrain has no last harmonic, so "how many leaves?" has no answer until you say to what tolerance, and then the count grows as a power of it rather than a logarithm.
9 not a closed curve 13 entries

The spiral

A logarithmic spiral grows by the same factor every turn, so it never closes, which makes it the most awkward thing to hand a format built on closed loops. Forced into one turn it does not converge at all: the error sits at half the gap, at every N. Give the growth to a scale factor instead and the circle keeps what is left, exactly, twenty turns out.
10 a rule, not a spectrum 13 entries

Differential growth

Every page before this one writes a spectrum down. Here a local rule runs instead (pull toward neighbours, push away from everything else, split long edges) and the spectrum is whatever comes out: coral, lettuce, kelp, cortex.
11 from specimens to scenes 13 entries

Assemblies

A rose window, an orrery, a gear train, a reef: hundreds of loops stacked in depth, filled and toned, in a format whose whole vocabulary is a single loop. What a scene costs, and why depth is painter's order.
12 type, traced 10 entries

Letters

Your browser's own fonts, traced into loops: an outline for each letter and one for every counter, the hole in an o or a B. Type is curves and corners, and the format prices them very differently: an O holds in 16 leaves, a W asks for hundreds. Weight is a blend, a slant is exact, and an o cannot become a c.
13 two loops, one edge 7 entries

Tiles

Escher's recipe: a polygon that tiles, each edge bent as the edge it meets. And the one thing a band-limited loop cannot do exactly: where three tiles meet, one must turn a corner, and these curves have none. The seam never closes, but slow the curve down at each corner and it shrinks by a power of N instead of a fixed factor.
14 a shape as a sound 11 entries

Heard

One channel of a closed curve is a single-cycle waveform, so a circle is a sine. Click a tile to hear it: the same numbers that draw the curve make the tone. The last section renders a band-limited and a naive player side by side and measures what folds back.
15 shapes in time 10 entries

The loop

Time is a circle too, so a loop has no seam for the same reason a closed curve has none. Keyframes sit at the leaf positions of the time circle and blend with the same kernel, so the band limit is the easing. Every tile runs; scrub any moment with its ring.
16 motion, assembled 12 entries

Composed

Bridges driving bridges: a rest shape, a global driver moving every leaf alike, and local drivers that each own a windowed neighbourhood, which is a shape with joints. It costs the sum of the parts rather than their product, and the bill at the bottom is both derived and measured.
17 the case against 11 entries

Where it breaks

The other pages make the case for the format; this one makes the case against it, and every number is measured in your tab. Corners that never arrive, the parity trap, morphs without correspondence, operators that do not commute, and, at the end, the claims that held.
and a bench sculpt, and keep the code

The playground

One shape and your hands. Drag a leaf and the curve follows; smooth it, add noise, symmetrize it, morph it toward a star. Underneath, the transcript writes down every gesture as the call that would have made it, against tvf.js, the library every page here is built on, so you leave with the code.
and then the other direction

The inspector

The seventeen pages go from a description to a shape. The inspector goes back: give it any closed curve, including one you sculpted in the editor (inspect the selection in its settings menu), and it reports the spectrum, the fold, the mirror and its axis, the symmetry group, the width and how few leaves would hold it, each linked to the page above that explains it.
Each page is one self-contained file that also works opened from disk. The leaf editor reads the shapes they send it; .tvf is the format both sides speak. The library itself is tvf.js, an ES module, the same code every page here carries inlined. The editor's own experiments are listed, each with a link that runs it, in the labs.
