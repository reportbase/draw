tvf shape space tvf.space.js

A shape with N leaves is 2N numbers, so a shape is a point, a family of shapes is a cloud of points, and everything linear algebra knows about clouds applies: a mean, a covariance, principal axes, a Gaussian to draw new members from. The catalogue's morph is the segment between two points. This page is the whole space they live in.

The question I built it to answer was whether the leaf basis is a good place to do statistics. It turns out the question is malformed, and finding that out was worth more than an answer. PCA is invariant under any orthogonal change of coordinates and the DFT is unitary, so the eigenvalues from leaf values and the eigenvalues from Fourier coefficients are the same numbers — measured below at 4×10⁻¹⁵. No representation choice at that level can matter. What does matter is alignment, and one of the four things you have to align is unique to a closed curve on a circle. The last section is where the linear model runs out, which it does for a reason worth seeing.

the cloud walking the basis where it breaks evidence
tvf.space.js is Procrustes alignment, PCA through the M×M Gram matrix, and a Jacobi eigensolver — no dependencies, because a page about closed curves should not have to load a linear algebra library to draw its own principal axes. Every mode is itself an ordinary Shape, so an eigenshape pastes into the leaf editor like anything else.
The one number to take away: leaf-basis and Fourier-basis PCA agree to 4×10⁻¹⁵, so the representation is not the variable — the alignment is.
