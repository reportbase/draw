A reader at a point holds, of each place it reads, one number: v/h. Dividing one extent by the other cancels the size of both, so one reader's numbers say which way every place lies and nothing about how far. This page shows how the figure comes back after that division, and what it costs.

Not a number per reading: another standpoint. A quotient fixes a line through its reader; two readers' lines towards the same place cross at the place. Three readers who know only the shape of their own triangle recover the figure exactly up to its size, and one length, how far apart two of them stand, gives the size. What has to be true is that two readers see each place, and know which of their readings it is. Every number is measured in your tab, and the readers can be dragged.

tvf.recovered.js is the recovery: places along an outline, each reader's quotient v/h of each place, the line a quotient fixes, crossing two readers' lines at the place (the pair meeting squarest, of those that see it), and the readers' own layout, in their units until one length is given. The same arithmetic as the leaf editor's undoing the division lab.
The number to carry off: what the division removes is one number for the whole figure, its size, and one length between two standpoints puts it back; the shape needs no number at all, only a second place to stand.
