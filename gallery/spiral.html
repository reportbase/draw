A logarithmic spiral is r = ebθ — the curve of pure self-similarity, where advancing one turn multiplies the radius by a constant, at every angle, exactly. It is also not a closed curve, which makes it the most awkward thing you can hand a format built on closed curves, and the most informative, because of precisely where it breaks.

The observer has two ladders and this curve needs both. The resolution ladder lives on the circle — angle, harmonics, the dyadic depth d = log₂N. The value-scale ladder lives on the logarithm — ⌊log₂|x|⌋, Mellin rather than a multiresolution nesting, and the papers are careful that these are not the same ladder. A log spiral lies along both: its angle is periodic and its radius is not, and log r is linear in θ. Force it into one turn and you are asking a circle to hold a ramp, which is a sawtooth, which has a jump — and the reconstruction then does not converge at all. Not slowly. Not at O(1/N). To a fixed error, forever. Put the growth on the ladder that was built for it and the circle keeps what is left, which turns out to be perfectly ordinary.
