tvf the corner tvf.corner.js

A reader reads v/h, how far across against how far along, and the corner is where the two are equal. Before it, v is read in terms of h; past it, h in terms of v; and the flip s ↦ 1/s turns one side into the other. Near and far are one geometry, read from either side.

This is the central result of Serial, Parallel and Nowhere (the reader-geometry paper in the wander repository). The page shows the flip, the sweep laid without trigonometry (proportion to the corner, then a level per doubling), what that does to the detail of a thing moving off, and why the corner is the cheapest place to be sure. Every number is measured in your tab; the first place can be dragged.

the flip proportion, then levels smaller, less detail the cheapest place
tvf.corner.js is the corner's arithmetic: the sweep g = (2/π)·atan(s) and its flip, the in-place sweep (proportion to the corner, then halvings), the level a reading falls in, the bands of detail a body of a given size shows at a distance, and the cost of a sure reading.
The number to carry off: g(s) + g(1/s) = 1, to the last digit: whatever a reader holds near, it holds the same way far, read from the other side.
