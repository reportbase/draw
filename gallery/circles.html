tvf circles draw anything with rotating circles

Any closed curve can be drawn by a chain of circles: each one rides on the rim of the one before and turns a whole number of times while the pen at the end goes round the shape once. Give it a heart and it finds the circles; give it a word, and one chain draws each letter. Take circles away and the drawing softens, which is exactly what you would want to know before using it.

The circles are the shape's Fourier coefficients (a circle's size is the coefficient's size, its speed the harmonic's number), so this is the same sum the epicycles page watches the format make from its leaves, here for anything you can draw. Smooth things need a handful; a corner needs a never-ending supply, and the page counts them.

the machine

pick a shape, take circles away

Pick a shape and watch the pen. The circles slider is how many of the biggest circles are kept: with one, a circle; with two, an ellipse; with a few, a heart; the star and the square need many before their corners stop being rounded. In a word, click a letter to see its own chain. The curve on the right is the error at each number of circles: a drop of a decade means ten times closer.

word into word

morph by mixing circles

Two words, letter for letter, mixed circle by circle: each letter's chain is a list of complex numbers, and a morph is the mix of two lists. There are no corners to pair and no vertex counts to match, and every halfway shape is a drawing the machine could have made. The price is the corners: halfway between two letters with square ends, the ends are soft. Fewer circles soften them further.

your own

draw a closed line

Draw any outline with a finger or the mouse and lift: it closes itself, and the circles that draw it appear. A loop with a self-crossing is fine; a hole is a second loop, which needs a chain of its own.

what it costs

circles needed, counted

The smallest number of circles that lands within 5%, 1% and 0.1% of the shape's own size (the drop in the error is exact at the 256 samples: the energy of the circles left out over the total). A circle is one circle and an ellipse two, because a circle is a single harmonic and an ellipse is two turning opposite ways. A heart drawn by its formula is exactly eight, since the formula is made of four harmonics. A square, a star or a letter with straight ends and sharp corners keeps asking: the last hundredth of a corner costs most of the circles.

A closed curve through N leaves is a chain of N − 1 circles, exactly, and the epicycles page is that fact as machinery; this page runs the same sum on outlines the format never held, and counts what dropping circles costs. The letters are traced from a bold sans-serif (the animated gallery's vector text), so the page needs no font.
