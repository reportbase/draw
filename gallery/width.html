tvf wheels that are not round tvf.width.js

A second chart on shape space. The other galleries write a curve as a radius — how far the boundary is from the centre at each angle. These are written by their support function h(θ): the distance from the centre to the tangent line whose outward normal points at θ. Same curves, different coordinates, and a family of facts that are invisible in one and one line in the other.

The width across θ is h(θ) + h(θ+π), and that sum cancels every odd harmonic and doubles every even one. So a curve has constant width if and only if its support function has no even harmonics above the constant — the whole theory of rollers, 20p coins and square-hole drills, as one sentence about a spectrum. Barbier's theorem and the convexity bound fall out of the same two lines, and both are measured on the page rather than quoted. Every tile is live; click the source to copy it.

Under every tile is its spectrum: bar m is how strongly the outline wobbles m times per lap, with m = 0 the round body itself. The scale is logarithmic, so a harmonic that is small but real is a short bar, and one that is exactly zero is a tick on the floor. For a group of loops it shows the largest one.

constant width machines evidence
tvf.width.js is built on the public surface of tvf.js — Shape, the primitives — and on tvf.shapes.js for the traced parts. Nothing here reaches into the kernel.
The curve comes out of the support function as p(θ) = h(θ)·(cos θ, sin θ) + h′(θ)·(−sin θ, cos θ), with radius of curvature ρ = h + h″. Sampling is uniform in the normal angle — not arc length, not the polar angle — so h up to harmonic H gives a curve up to H + 1, and the leaf requirement is 2·(H + 1) + 1. h′ is summed in closed form rather than differenced: the derivative of a finite trig series is another finite trig series, and a finite difference would put an error on every leaf for nothing.
Barbier 1860 (perimeter πw), Blaschke 1915 and Lebesgue 1914 (least area), Reuleaux 1875. The 20p and 50p are Reuleaux heptagons; an even-sided Reuleaux polygon does not exist, since the construction needs a vertex opposite every arc.
