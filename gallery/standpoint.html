tvf where to stand tvf.stand.js

A reader that holds a shape as depth stands on its outline and reads it from there. A perfect circle, read along its tangent, holds nothing: the reader's own unit circle is the shape. The editor's circle cost 810 sweeps all the same. This page is why: near home, a reader reads its own neighbourhood, magnified by the levels.

Every outline drawn from samples ripples between them, by far less than an eye could see. Where the reader stands on that ripple decides what it reads near home: where the ripple crosses its mean, its slope turns the reader's facing; at a crest, its bend is the reading. Either way a ripple a ten-thousandth of the radius high becomes a departure of tens of per cent, and hundreds of sweeps. Every number is measured in your tab.

a turned facing standing on a ripple what it costs
tvf.stand.js is the model: a circle with a ripple R(θ) = 1 + a·cos(mθ), a reader standing anywhere on it facing one of several ways, the departure it reads at every address, and the depth file that holds it, written with the editor's own depth code, copied unchanged.
The number to carry off: a ripple 9×10⁻⁵ of the radius high, eighty to the turn, read where it crosses its mean, gives a departure of 0.53; the editor's circle measured 0.54. A perfect circle costs one sweep.
